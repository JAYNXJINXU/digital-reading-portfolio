## Title of Reading Selection
    `Computational co-creation in digital media art: adaptive aesthetic intelligence for interactive visual expression`

<br>

<h5>
The article by Yu Liu, Haozhe Bai, and Yunlong Li (2026) explains how artificial intelligence can work with people to create interactive digital art. The Adaptive
Aesthetic Intelligence (AAI) system they presented changes artwork based on the input of a user. However, I do disagree with the use of an AI model as a so-called
"creative partner" even if it is convenient because I believe art is an expression of human ideas and emotions that is unique to each person. The author presents a 96.8%
realism score and an 18.9% improvement in responsiveness, but these results measure its technical performance rather than the personal expression in the artwork. Simply
looking similar to human made art doesn't necessarily mean that the work has any originality or meaning. The comparisons they presented with other systems do
strengthen their reasoning, but they do not fully explain why AI should be considered a creative partner. The tables do help present the findings, but the technical terms
and equations make certain sections harder to understand. The authors show objectivity by admitting that their main dataset may not represent the different artistic styles
and cultures. This limitation strengthens my concern that the system may miss the cultural and personal experience tied behind the art. Overall, the article shows what AI
can do in terms of digital media, but it does not convince me that AI can express the ideas and emotions found in human art.
</h5>
